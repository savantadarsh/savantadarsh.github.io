Connecting product-level behaviour, conversion funnels and repeat purchasing to actionable retail opportunities.

The commercial question

For a multi-category online retailer, the objective was to understand what customers were doing across the shopping journey, where purchase intent was breaking down, and which changes were most likely to improve commercial performance.

The work brought together conversion funnel analysis, customer value, repeat behaviour and product-category reporting. Underlying client datasets and internal commercial documents are not shared here.

How I approached it

  1. Defined decision-oriented metrics: product views, add-to-cart behaviour, purchase progression, repeat purchase, customer value and category attribution quality.
  2. Segmented customers: used recency, frequency and value-based analysis to distinguish recent one-time purchasers from stronger repeat segments.
  3. Analysed category-level friction: compared product engagement and cart progression to identify areas where shopping confidence or product experience merited investigation.
  4. Checked measurement reliability: investigated incomplete product-category classification so that merchandising decisions were based on dependable attribution.

Insights & recommended actions

01 — Apparel purchase confidence

Recommended more helpful personalised clothing-size guidance as a targeted response to friction in apparel progression.

02 — Furniture product experience

Recommended exploring augmented-reality visualisation to help customers judge fit, scale and suitability before purchase.

03 — Repeat-purchase opportunity

Defined a recently active, single-purchase customer segment for RFM-triggered next-purchase communications.

04 — Category data quality

Proposed a governed product-to-category enrichment approach to improve the reliability of category-level reporting while preserving source data.

Good commercial recommendations depend on both customer insight and confidence in the underlying measurements.

Outcome & boundaries

All four recommendations were accepted by the client for consideration before implementation. This case study therefore describes analysis and recommended actions, not uplift attributable to those recommendations.

Confidentiality: This is a high-level, anonymised description of professional client work. Detailed transaction data, commercially sensitive metrics, dashboards and client identities have been omitted.

What this demonstrates

Commercial problem framing, customer and product analytics, validation of measurement quality, prioritisation of actions, and clear stakeholder communication.
